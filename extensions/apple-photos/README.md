# Apple Photos

Browse your recent Apple Photos in a grid, jump into any album, and paste the latest photo directly into any app.

## Commands

### Search Recent Photos

Opens a grid of your most recent photos from Apple Photos, sorted newest first.

- **Copy to Clipboard** (↵) — Copies the full-resolution JPEG to your clipboard
- **Paste to [App]** (⌘↵) — Copies and pastes directly into the app you were using before opening Raycast
- **Open in Photos** (⌘O) — Reveals the photo in the Photos app
- **Quick Look** (⌘Y) — Preview the photo without leaving Raycast
- **Refresh** (⌘R) — Reload with the latest photos from your library

### Go to Albums

Shows a grid of all of your user albums with the album's key photo as cover and its photo count. Smart albums show a gear icon (Photos can't assign them a key photo), and regular albums without a key photo show a stacked-rectangle icon. Albums are grouped into sections: top-level albums first, one section per folder, and the Trash album last. Press ↵ to jump straight into the album in Photos.

- **Open in Photos** (↵) — Opens Photos and navigates into the selected album
- **Copy Album Name** — Copies the album name to your clipboard
- **Refresh** (⌘R) — Reload the album list

### Paste Recent Photo

A no-UI command that instantly pastes your most recent photo into the frontmost app. Assign a hotkey for the fastest possible workflow.

## Preferences

| Preference       | Default | Description                                |
| ---------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Number of Photos | 24      | How many recent photos to show in the grid |

## Notes

- **First load**: Thumbnails are generated by exporting from Photos and resizing with `sips`. This takes a few seconds on the first run; subsequent opens are instant from cache. Album covers use the same cache.
- **Permissions**: macOS will prompt for Photos access the first time the extension runs. Grant access to allow the extension to read your library. Opening albums relies on Photos' own AppleScript support, so no Accessibility permission is needed.
- Photos are always copied/pasted as full-resolution JPEGs, regardless of the original format (HEIC, PNG, etc.).
